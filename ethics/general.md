# Ethics

Software engineering ethics involve a developer's responsibility for the social effects of systems they create. Ethical considerations guide decision-making throughout the software lifecycle, from design and development to deployment and maintenance, ensuring that technology serves the public good, minimizes harm, and removes [biases](/ethics/biases.md)

## Key Ethical Concerns

- **Intellectual Property:** Respecting copyrights, licenses, and patents; avoiding plagiarism and unauthorized use of code, assets, or proprietary technologies; understanding open-source licensing obligations.
- **Data Privacy:** Protecting users' personal information from unauthorized access or exposure; implementing appropriate security measures; being transparent about data collection and usage practices.
- **Data Minimisation:** Collecting only the data that is strictly necessary for a system to function; avoiding excessive or unnecessary data gathering; ensuring data retention policies align with privacy principles.

## Ethics Code

There are ethics codes determined by ethical [bodies](/ethics/bodies.md) such as the ACM and IEEE. These codes establish professional standards of conduct, covering principles like public interest, client and employer obligations, product quality, professional judgment, and professional development.

## LESCANT Model

The LESCANT model is a framework for understanding cross-cultural factors that can affect software engineering and communication in global teams. It identifies seven key dimensions:

- **L - Language:** Differences in language, jargon, idioms, and communication styles that can lead to misunderstandings.
- **E - Environment and Technology:** Variations in technological infrastructure, tools, and work environments across cultures.
- **S - Social Organization:** How social structures, hierarchies, and group dynamics influence teamwork and decision-making.
- **C - Contexting:** The degree to which communication relies on explicit words (low-context) versus implicit context and shared understanding (high-context).
- **A - Authority Conception:** Different attitudes toward authority, leadership, and hierarchical decision-making.
- **N - Nonverbal Behavior:** Body language, gestures, eye contact, and other nonverbal cues that carry different meanings across cultures.
- **T - Time Conception:** Differences in how cultures view time — monochronic (linear, schedules-focused) versus polychronic (flexible, multi-tasking).
