# Kanban

Kanban is an [agile](/processes/agile.md) framework focused on visualizing work, limiting work in progress (WIP), and maximizing efficiency. It helps teams identify bottlenecks, reduce waste, and continuously improve their workflow. Kanban is highly adaptable and can be applied to almost any type of work or existing process.

## Core Principles of Kanban

*   **Visualize the Workflow:** Make all work items and their current status visible, typically using a Kanban board.
*   **Limit Work in Progress (WIP):** Restrict the number of tasks in each stage of the workflow to prevent overburdening the team and improve focus.
*   **Manage Flow:** Track, measure, and optimize the flow of work through the system to ensure smooth and predictable delivery.
*   **Make Process Policies Explicit:** Clearly define and communicate the rules, guidelines, and definitions for each stage of the workflow.
*   **Implement Feedback Loops:** Regularly review the workflow and processes to identify areas for improvement and adapt as needed.
*   **Improve Collaboratively, Evolve Experimentally:** Encourage continuous improvement through shared understanding and data-driven evolutionary change.

## Board

The Kanban board is the central component of this software development workflow. It provides a visual representation of all work items, typically categorized into columns such as "To Do," "In Progress," "Review," and "Done." Each work item, often represented as a card, moves through these stages from left to right, providing a clear overview of the project's status and progress. The board helps teams to:

*   **See the Big Picture:** Understand the entire workflow at a glance.
*   **Identify Bottlenecks:** Easily spot where work is accumulating.
*   **Improve Transparency:** Ensure everyone knows what others are working on.
*   **Facilitate Communication:** Provide a common visual language for team discussions.
